The CardVault 50 is a custom index tracker that works like a stock market index for your collection. You pick up to 50 cards, CardVault normalizes their combined value to **100 points** at the moment of your first snapshot, and every subsequent snapshot shows you whether your chosen cards have collectively risen or fallen from that baseline. Access it via **Analytics → CardVault 50**.

---

## How the Index Works

The CardVault 50 uses a **divisor methodology** — the same mechanism major stock indexes use to stay continuous across component changes.

### Initial Snapshot

When you take your very first snapshot, CardVault calculates a divisor:

```
divisor = total_value_of_all_50_cards / 100
```

From that point on, the index value is always:

```
index_value = total_value_of_all_50_cards / divisor
```

Because the divisor is set so that the initial total equals 100, the index opens at exactly **100.00**.

<Note>
  The CardVault 50 KPI tile on the Dashboard shows **—** (an em dash) until you have taken at least one snapshot. The index has no value until there is a reference point to measure from.
</Note>

### Adding or Removing Cards

When you add or remove a card from the index, CardVault **recalculates the divisor** before the change takes effect in the next snapshot. This keeps the index continuous — the index value does not jump simply because you swapped out a component, just as the S&P 500 does not reset to zero when a company is added.

```
new_divisor = new_total_value / current_index_value
```

This means historical snapshots remain meaningful and comparable even after you change the composition of your index.

## Managing Your Index Cards

### Searching and Adding Cards

From the CardVault 50 page, use the search bar to find cards by player name, set, or card ID. Click **Add** on any result to include it in the index. CardVault enforces the **50-card cap** — once you have 50 components, the Add button is disabled for all search results until you remove a card.

### Removing Cards

Each card in your current index list has a **Remove** button. Removing a card adjusts the divisor immediately so the index value remains continuous at the next snapshot.

<Warning>
  Removing a card from the index is permanent for historical purposes — past snapshots that included that card's value are frozen and do not change, but future snapshots will no longer include it.
</Warning>

## Taking Snapshots

Snapshots are **manual** — CardVault does not automatically record the index value on a schedule. You decide when to capture a moment in time.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Review your current index composition">
    Confirm that the cards listed are the ones you want tracked. Check that all 50 slots are filled if you want maximum resolution.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Take the snapshot">
    Click the **Take Snapshot** button on the CardVault 50 page. CardVault computes the current total value of your index components, divides by the divisor, and records the resulting index value along with the timestamp.
  </Step>
  <Step title="View it on the chart">
    The new data point appears immediately on the historical chart. Take snapshots regularly — weekly or after major price lookups — to build a meaningful time series.
  </Step>
</Steps>

---

## The Historical Chart

The chart plots every snapshot you have taken as a connected line, with the index value on the y-axis and the snapshot date on the x-axis. It gives you a visual record of how your personally curated 50 cards have performed over time relative to your own baseline.

## Choosing the Right Cards

<Tip>
  For the most meaningful index movement, populate your CardVault 50 with cards that have **market-priced values** — specifically cards priced via Card Ladder or eBay lookup. Cards priced by estimate or a default pricing rule tend to hold flat values and will **dampen real movement** in the index, making it harder to detect genuine market trends in your collection.
</Tip>

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Good Index Candidates" icon="circle-check">
    Cards with recent eBay sold comps, graded cards with active populations, rookie cards of active players — anything with a price that genuinely responds to the market.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Poor Index Candidates" icon="circle-xmark">
    Commons carrying a flat default price, vintage cards with no recent sales priced by estimate only, or duplicates of the same card type.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>
