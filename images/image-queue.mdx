The Image Queue gives you a structured, distraction-free workflow for building out your card image library. Rather than hunting down images card by card, the queue presents one **card type** at a time — walking you through every set that still needs coverage — so you can make steady, measurable progress across your entire 56,000+ card collection.

## How the Queue Works

The Image Queue operates on **card types**, not individual copies. A card type is identified by its `uniq_id` and category combination — so if you own three copies of the same Luka Dončić rookie, pasting one image pair covers all three at once. This makes the workflow dramatically faster for collectors with duplicate cards.

The queue groups card types **by set**, presenting all unimaged cards within a set before moving to the next one. A **set** is defined by the five-column combination of category, brand, release year, set name, and card type — so two products that share a name but differ in category or brand are treated as separate sets. A progress indicator shows **N of M saved** so you always know where you stand within the current set.

<Note>
  CardVault never contacts external sites on your behalf. You find and copy images yourself using your own browser — CardVault only receives what you paste with Ctrl+V.
</Note>

## Opening the Image Queue

Navigate to the Image Queue from the **Quick Actions** sidebar on any page. The queue automatically loads the first card type that is missing a front image, ready for input.

## The Paste Workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Image Queue">
    Click **Image Queue** in the Quick Actions sidebar. CardVault loads the first unimaged card type, showing the card's details and two empty image slots — front and back.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find and copy the card image">
    In a separate browser tab, locate the card image on [TCDB](https://www.tcdb.com), eBay, or any other card reference site. Right-click the image and choose **Copy Image** (or use your OS copy shortcut).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Paste into CardVault">
    Return to the CardVault tab and press **Ctrl+V** anywhere on the page. The pasted image fills the **front slot** first. Press Ctrl+V again with a second image to fill the **back slot**. You do not need to click inside any input field — the paste listener is global.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Crop the image (optional)">
    If the image needs trimming, click the **✂ crop button** beneath the filled slot to open the crop tool. Adjust the crop handles to align with the card borders, then confirm. See [Crop Tool](#crop-tool) below for full details.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review multi-copy behavior">
    If you own more than one copy of this card type, the checkbox **"Use these images for all N copies"** appears and is checked by default. Leave it checked to apply the same front/back images to every copy simultaneously. Uncheck it only if you need different images for a specific copy.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save and advance">
    Click **Save & Next** or press **Ctrl+Enter**. CardVault saves the images and immediately loads the next unimaged card type in the set. The progress counter updates to reflect the save.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Navigation Controls

Not every card in the queue needs an image right now. Two navigation actions let you control your pace without losing your place.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Skip Card" icon="forward">
    Moves past the current card type without saving. The card remains in the queue and will reappear the next time you open it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Next Set" icon="layer-group">
    Skips all remaining unimaged cards in the current set and jumps to the first card of the next set within the same category. Useful when you don't have images for a particular product line handy.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Save Front Only & Next

If you have a front image but no back image readily available, click **Save Front Only & Next**. CardVault saves the front image, marks the card as imaged, and advances to the next card — the saved card will no longer appear in the main queue pass. Back images can be added later from the [card detail panel](/images/uploading-images).

## Jumping to a Specific Set

<Tip>
  Use the **set picker** to jump directly to any set you want to work on, rather than working through the queue in default order. The picker is a two-step selector: choose the **category** first (e.g., Basketball, Hockey), then choose the **set** from the filtered list. This is especially useful when you've just received cards from a specific product line and want to image that set first.
</Tip>

## Progress Display

The queue header shows **N of M saved** for the current set, where N is the number of card types with at least a front image and M is the total card types in the set. This count updates in real time as you save, giving you an at-a-glance measure of set completion.

## Crop Tool

When you click the ✂ button after pasting an image, the crop tool opens as an overlay with the following options:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Orientation modes">
    The crop tool supports two locked aspect ratios matching standard trading card dimensions:

    | Mode | Aspect Ratio | Use When |
    |---|---|---|
    | **Portrait** | 2.5 : 3.5 | Standard upright card orientation |
    | **Landscape** | 3.5 : 2.5 | Horizontal card design |

    The tool **defaults to the orientation that matches the pasted image** — a tall image opens in Portrait mode; a wide image opens in Landscape mode. You can switch between Portrait and Landscape mid-session without losing the pasted image.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Free-form mode">
    Toggle **Free-form** to remove the aspect ratio lock entirely. This lets you crop to any arbitrary rectangle, which can be useful for non-standard card shapes or when correcting a heavily skewed scan. Free-form crops are stored at whatever dimensions you set.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Pixel dimension readout">
    A live **pixel dimension readout** beneath the crop area shows the exact output size as you drag the handles. 

    <Warning>
      If either dimension drops below **200 px**, the readout turns red as a warning. Very small images display poorly in the card detail panel and may produce low-quality prints. Aim for at least 400 px on the short edge for best results.
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

After adjusting the crop, click **Apply** to confirm. The cropped result replaces the raw paste in the image slot. You can re-open the crop tool and adjust again before saving.
